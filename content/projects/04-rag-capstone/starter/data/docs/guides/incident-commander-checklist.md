# Guide: Incident Commander Checklist

The incident commander coordinates a P1 incident. They do not fix the problem themselves: they make sure the right people are working on it, and that everyone else knows what is happening.

## In the first 15 minutes

1. Confirm the priority with the on-call engineer who opened the incident.
2. Open an incident channel, and invite the owning team's on-call engineers.
3. Post on the public status page if customers are affected, as the incident communication policy requires.
4. Assign a scribe to keep the incident timeline.

## While the incident is open

- Ask for a status summary every 30 minutes, and update the status page at the same rhythm.
- Approve emergency changes, and make sure each one has a rollback plan.
- Bring in other teams early. It is cheaper to release an engineer who wasn't needed than to wait for one who was.
- Watch for fatigue: after four hours, hand over to the next incident commander on duty.

## Closing the incident

Declare the incident resolved only when the service has been healthy for 30 minutes. Then post a final status page update, and schedule the postmortem within five working days.
